About me

I'm a web developer who thinks in systems — a habit shaped as much by books and games as by code. Reading gave me patience for structure. Every great story is architecture: setup, rising tension, payoff. I bring that same instinct to building interfaces — thinking about how a user moves through a page the way a novelist paces a chapter, making sure nothing feels rushed or out of place. Non-fiction sharpens the analytical side, feeding a habit of breaking big, messy problems into clear, logical pieces before I ever touch a keyboard. Gaming taught me to obsess over feedback loops. Good games reward curiosity and make failure feel useful, not frustrating. I carry that philosophy into UX — designing interactions that respond intuitively, guide users without hand-holding, and turn small moments (a hover, a transition, a loading state) into something satisfying rather than forgettable. Debugging, too, feels a lot like leveling up: iterate, adjust, try again. Web development is where these threads meet. I write clean, functional code, but I care just as much about the experience wrapped around it — the narrative flow of a site, the "game feel" of its interactions. Books taught me to tell a story. Games taught me to make it interactive. Code lets me build both.
